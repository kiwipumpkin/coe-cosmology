{\bfseries Abstract\par}\end{center}\vspace{-2pt}
\normalsize
This collection asks whether non-accelerating expansion in a finite, off-centre geometry can look accelerated to an observer who is not at the Centre of Expansion (CoE). The first paper builds a CoE-plus-thick-shell geometry from observables, derives its angular morphology before comparing it with the independent Migkas et al.\ cluster anisotropy $A/A_{\rm all}$, and finds that the pure geometry overshoots the $\sim10^{-5}$ CMB isotropy level at last scattering. Special-relativistic Doppler plus central-mass gravitational redshift cancel 95.9\% of the geometric RMS while keeping the low-$z$ morphology; residual multipoles, a CatWISE/Secrest matter dipole ($D\approx0.0155$) and redshift-dependent cancellation $A(z,\theta)$ are examined. In this draft, apparent acceleration is interpreted as an off-centre sampling effect. A supplement repeats the anisotropy calculation exactly in a Lema\^{\i}tre--Tolman--Bondi dust model. With no mass it returns the Milne value $H_0=70.00$ in every direction, and in the classical limit it matches the classical values to about $10^{-3}$. The $0^\circ$ bump grows with central mass and with closeness to the CoE, the minimum moves from about $120^\circ$ toward $105^\circ$, and the $180^\circ$ rise reaches 1.02--1.14. The sky mean never reaches Planck or SH0ES, but at $R_{\rm obs}=3$\,Gpc individual directions span both when $2GM_c/c^2\gtrsim(0.3$--$1)\,D$.

The second paper treats the leftover CMB residual (237 times the $10^{-5}$ level) as a fingerprint and asks which arrangements of masses and voids cancel it. Outside the visible sphere 11 bodies (6 masses, 5 voids) reach the target and inside it 4--8 suffice. Editing 12\% of a balanced random web brings the residual from $789\times$ to $1.00\times$ without forming a ring. The third paper classifies twenty spontaneous patterns into five classes by how their scale is selected, giving each its forcing function, threshold and selected scale. It then applies the thin-shell (Vishniac) instability to the CoE onion, where wrinkled shells seed clumps and voids. A closing note argues that every observed physical system has a geometric, mass or momentum centre, and that proposed counter-examples have so far been mathematical abstractions. All results are preliminary.
