## Primary objective

Understand the **cosmological principle (CP)** as a physical assumption — what it asserts, what it does not, how it enters the Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (**FLRW**) metric, and what would pressure or falsify it — **through a continuous Center-of-Expansion (CoE) / off-center project thread** that runs Modules 1–9. The off-center observer in a spherical decreasing-density expansion (CoE) is the **spine**: every module forces you to apply that module’s concept to the same geometry (\(D\), \(r\), \(R_{\mathrm{CMB}}\), Gauss/shell, dipole budget, Migkas/Secrest claims-with-caveats, Alnes–Kodama, kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (**kSZ**)). Broader cosmology is taught in service of CP understanding; the CoE sketch is the clarifying case, not a finale bolt-on and not advocacy.

## You should be able to

1. **State the CP cleanly.** Separate homogeneity from isotropy; separate the Copernican slogan (“we are not special” / “no center”) from the mathematical symmetry FLRW actually uses — using the decreasing-\(\rho\) off-center sketch as the clarifying counter-example.
2. **Say what “large scales” means — and where it is slippery.** Know that “averaging until FLRW appears” is an operational claim, not a theorem, and that different tracers and different scales can disagree.
3. **Read FLRW as a symmetry ansatz.** Recognize what Killing-vector / metric-form assumptions encode (no preferred center), and state explicitly what a CoE ansatz *breaks*.
4. **Distinguish what data constrain.** Isotropy about *us* is not homogeneity of space; most sky maps test the former unless a strong reconstruction argument is added.
5. **Carry the project geometry everywhere.** Symbols \(D\) (CoE→us), \(r\) (vision shell), \(R_{\mathrm{CMB}}\gg D\) (last-scattering / cosmic microwave background (**CMB**) shell); decreasing density (not uniform ball); Gauss/shell; dipole budget \((\delta T/T)_{\mathrm{geom}}\approx\alpha(D/R_{\mathrm{CMB}})(\Delta H/H)_{\mathrm{eff}}\) with \(D/R_{\mathrm{CMB}}\lesssim 0.02\) for Migkas-sized contrast (\(\sim 0.06\)) if geom dipole \(\lesssim 1.2\times10^{-3}\).
6. **Use late-universe isotropy tests as physics, not scorekeeping.** Clusters (Migkas \(L_X\)–\(T\) / directional \(H\)), Type Ia supernovae (**SNIa**), radio/quasar dipoles (Secrest), and the CMB each measure different things; systematics and interpretation matter. Amp\(\times\sin\) / \(2\ell\) / \(R^2\) to digitised Migkas are **diagnostics**, not proofs.
7. **State Alnes–Kodama and kSZ with correct scope.** Alnes & Amarzguioui (2006) \(\sim 15\,\mathrm{Mpc}\) for order-unity Gpc Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (**LTB**) voids; Kodama-class tens of Mpc still \(\ll\) Gpc; smaller-contrast arithmetic differs; kSZ (García-Bellido & Haugbølle 2008) pressures deep voids even on-center.
8. **Know when mean-field cosmology can fail.** Averaging and backreaction (Buchert, Wiltshire/Bolejko territory): ask whether a CoE sketch needs backreaction language or explicit inhomogeneity.
9. **Defend one falsifiable claim in a Module 9 final paper.** Observable + dataset + pass/fail; Alnes–Kodama + kSZ stated plainly; synthesis of project threads 1–8 — not a standalone “design a test from scratch” capstone bolted on after the fact.

## Explicit non-goals of this course pack

- Cheerleading ΛCDM, or cheerleading any Center-of-Expansion research program. Same evidentiary standard both ways.
- Replacing peer-reviewed sources with thesis rhetoric; inventing citations.
- Teaching you to “win” debates. Teaching you to state assumptions, map observables to geometry, and name tensions without hand-waving.
