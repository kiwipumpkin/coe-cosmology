## 1. Goal

Separate the local linear velocity–distance relation from cosmological expansion history. Understand **bulk flows** as coherent peculiar motion that can mimic directional \(H\) variation — including the \(\sim 900\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) Migkas alternative. Separate that degeneracy from a true CoE-driven expansion anisotropy.

---

## 2. Chem / applied-physics bridge

Kinetic theory vs hydro: local random motions vs organized flow. A bulk flow is hydro-like; shot noise in galaxy peculiar velocities is kinetic-theory-like. A CoE directional expansion is a **third** story (background geometry / expansion varies with direction) that must be separated from both.

---

## 3. Local Hubble law vs cosmic expansion

Locally, observers often write \(v \approx H_0 d\) for recession velocity vs distance. Two meanings get confused:

- **Definition / estimator:** fit a slope \(H_0\) to a local sample (supernovae (**SN**), galaxies, …).
- **Prediction:** FLRW (from the **cosmological principle (CP)**) predicts a specific expansion history \(H(z)\) once parameters are fixed.

On small scales, peculiar velocities dominate. On large scales, the Hubble flow dominates — if the **Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW)** background is adequate.

---

## 4. Bulk flows

A **bulk flow** is a coherent peculiar velocity of a large region relative to the cosmic microwave background (**CMB**) frame. It can imprint a dipole in redshift at fixed distance, which an isotropic-FLRW analysis can misread as directional \(H\).

**Migkas et al. (2021),** *A&A* **649**, A151: apparent \(\sim 9\%\) spatial variation in local \(H_0\) (nearly dipole); **alternatively** interpretable as a \(\sim 900\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}\) bulk flow out to \(\sim 500\,\mathrm{Mpc}\). That alternative is mandatory course vocabulary — not optional fine print.

---

## 5. Project thread — bulk flow vs CoE degeneracy

| Story | What varies on the sky | Rough kill / break |
|-------|------------------------|--------------------|
| Bulk flow | Peculiar velocity field; dipole in \(z\) at fixed \(d\) | Redshift dependence; fades or matches velocity-field reconstructions; apex vs CMB kinematic dipole |
| True directional \(H\) (CoE-like) | Expansion / distance–redshift mapping with axis toward CoE | Persists in independent samples; not absorbed by velocity models; must still pass CMB dipole budget (Module 6) |
| Calibration / absorption fake | Survey systematics | Stress tests; multi-wavelength cross-checks (Module 5) |

A CoE sketch with \(D\neq 0\) *predicts* a preferred axis. A bulk flow *also* predicts a preferred axis. Same sky dipole shape does **not** decide which story is true. Module 5–7 add systematics, CMB budget, Alnes–Kodama, and kSZ.
