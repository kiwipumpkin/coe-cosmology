## 1. Goal

Understand Buchert averaging / backreaction language and Wiltshire–Bolejko-type arguments for when a smooth **Friedmann–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW)** “mean field” can misrepresent the real volume average. Ask whether a CoE sketch needs backreaction language or **explicit** inhomogeneity (**Lemaître–Tolman–Bondi (LTB)**-class).

---

## 2. Chem / applied-physics bridge

Mean-field theory in statistical mechanics: replacing \(\langle n^2\rangle\) by \(\langle n\rangle^2\) fails near critical points and in strongly inhomogeneous media. Backreaction is the gravity version of asking whether that replacement is safe. Averaging the density is **not** the same as averaging the metric.

---

## 3. When mean-field FLRW can fail

The **cosmological principle (CP)** licenses an FLRW background after coarse-graining. Structure means light travels through voids and walls differently; clocks and rulers can be environment-dependent in some proposals (timescape / Wiltshire territory).

**Wiltshire (2007),** *Phys. Rev. Lett.* **99**, 251101; **Wiltshire (2009),** *Phys. Rev. D* **80**, 123512 — averaging / timescape observational quantities.

**Bolejko & Korzyński (2017),** *Int. J. Mod. Phys. D* **26**, 1730011; arXiv:1612.08222 — status review: inhomogeneous cosmology and backreaction; claims require care; community is divided on magnitude.

Backreaction asks: can the volume average of an inhomogeneous dust spacetime behave unlike FLRW with the same mean density? That is a precise **general relativity (GR)** question — not a license to ignore the **cosmic microwave background (CMB)**, **kinematic Sunyaev–Zeldovich (kSZ)**, or Alnes–Kodama when you also posit a spherical CoE.

---

## 4. Project thread — backreaction vs explicit CoE

| Approach | What it assumes | Fit to CoE spine? |
|----------|-----------------|-------------------|
| FLRW + perturbations | CP background; small deviations | No preferred center in the background |
| Buchert / backreaction | Domain averages; effective equations | Usually **no** single preferred center; statistical inhomogeneity |
| Explicit LTB / CoE | Real center; \(\rho(r)\); observer at \(D\) | **Yes** — preferred center is primitive |

A CoE with symbols \(D\), \(r\), \(R_{\mathrm{CMB}}\) and a heating dipole along a preferred axis is naturally an **explicit inhomogeneous metric** problem (Module 7), not a “mean FLRW plus small Buchert correction” slogan. You *may* discuss whether some averaged description approximates the same observables — but that is extra work, not a substitute for the CMB dipole budget or kSZ.

## 7. Takeaway

Mean-field FLRW can fail in principle; magnitude is contested. The CoE spine is usually **explicit inhomogeneity**, not a backreaction slogan. Module 9 reviews the whole spine and asks for one falsifiable claim.
