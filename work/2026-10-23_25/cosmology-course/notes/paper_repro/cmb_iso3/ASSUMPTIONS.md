# cmb_iso3 (mixed: expansion + peculiar < c): isotropic emission at R_rec crossing (assumptions)
Sources: /tmp/a.txt (Chris's paper text): D = 58.2 Gpc, annulus D−d..D+d = 43.9..72.5 Gpc (lines 285–287; CatWISE-locked fit value, used as geometry); vision radius R = 14.3 Gpc (line 164, "fixed ... not fitted"); Milne age t0 = 13.97 Gyr (line 1121). R_rec is NOT given numerically in the paper; 24.5 Mpc is taken from earlier chat work (stretch-to-2.725 K estimate), not from the paper.
A1 Kinematics: everything coasts from the CoE at t=0, v = r/t. Matter with speed v crosses R_rec at t_c = R_rec/v and is now at r_now = vT.
A2 Emission: each matter element releases 3000 K photons isotropically once, at its R_rec crossing event (position R_rec·n̂, time t_c). The R_rec sphere does not shine as a wall.
A4 Ray trace: for each sky direction, find every t_c where the backward ray from the observer (at D now) meets the comoving image of the R_rec sphere. Each root is an emission event, with emit time t_c, receive time T, and travel delay T − t_c recorded.
A5 Weight per event: (density at crossing ρ_now·λ³) × (crossing speed v) / |d(miss)/dt_c| (thin-surface heuristic), λ = r_now/R_rec. Laws are defined in r at ALL radii, with a 3% lock between 43.9 and 72.5 Gpc: power ρ∝r^−k (k=ln1.03/ln(72.5/43.9)), A+B/r³, linear (quadratic γ=0) about D clipped ≥0, step = 1 for r≥43.9 and 5 inside, and uniform.
A7 Direction temperature: bolometric blend T_dir = (Σ w T_i⁴ / Σ w)^{1/4} (intensity-weighted, blackbody). No overall brightness normalisation.
A8 Stats (sinθ-weighted mean, cosθ dipole, rms residual) only when every direction is defined. Otherwise "partial sky, stats not computed". Plots keep the dipole.
A9 Masses 0, 1e12, 1e15, 1e18, 1e20 M☉ = mass inside the annulus 43.9–72.5 Gpc. Φ(r) = −G M_enc(r)/r (enclosed mass only; outer shells dropped, so not exact). Mass does not decelerate the kinematics.
A10 Ray grid t_c from 1e-60·T to T (cmb_iso stopped at 1e-12·T, which wrongly gave f=0.25 a dark sky: the crossings are at about 1e-13 Gyr). Hard assert: for every f>0 every direction's ray meets the R_rec crossing surface (photon field present). This passes.
A3' Total recession of matter = r/t (coasting, can exceed c). The observer's peculiar (through-space) speed is βc with β ∈ {0, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5, 0.9}, directed radially outward. The rest is expansion. A homogeneous expansion H(t) = F/t, i.e. a ∝ t^F, with F = 1 − βc/v_obs (v_obs = D/T = 13.62c) reproduces the observer's split exactly: F = 1, 0.9926, 0.9779, 0.9632, 0.9338.
A3'' Emitter peculiar speed = β·min(1, v/v_obs)·c, radial (my choice, as suggested). Light propagation and stretch use the homogeneous a ∝ t^F, so for emitters faster than the observer, total = expansion + peculiar is only approximate: the expansion part is F·v, not v − β_e c. Error fraction ≤ β/13.6 for the observer and smaller for fast emitters.
A6' Pieces per photon: travel delay T − t_c; stretch (T/t_c)^F (cube root of volume growth under a ∝ t^F); SR Doppler on peculiar velocities ν_o/ν_e = γ_o(1 − β_o·k̂)/[γ_s(1 − β_s·k̂)], always defined since all peculiar speeds < c; weak-field Newtonian gravity as in A9, with events rejected if 1 + ΔΦ/c² ≤ 0 (flagged).
A12 Hard assert: every sky direction's ray meets the R_rec crossing surface for every β. This passes. Directions lose light only when weak-field gravity fails (M ≥ 1e18 M☉), which is a failure of the gravity treatment, not missing photons.
A13 Kinematic dipole cancellation: an extra observer boost β_b along the CoE axis, using exact SR (Doppler factor plus aberration of the computed sky), solved so the l=1 Legendre coefficient of the boosted sky is zero. The sky is axisymmetric, so no transverse component is needed. Residuals are rms(ΔT/T) after a Legendre fit removing l≤1, after removing l≤2, and after the boost (l≤1 removed). Full curves are never modified.
A14 Best boost (supersedes the dipole-only goal): an exact SR transform of the whole computed sky, T'(n') = T(n)/[γ(1 − β n'·v̂)] with aberration n ↔ n'. β is chosen to minimise the TOTAL rms anisotropy of the transformed sky (bounded search |β| < 0.99). The direction is restricted to the CoE axis, because the raw sky is axisymmetric and any transverse boost breaks that symmetry and adds anisotropy (argued, not numerically scanned). The multipole breakdown a1..a4 is the Legendre coefficients of ΔT/T. The boosted skies are in *_boosted.png. Raw curves are unmodified.
