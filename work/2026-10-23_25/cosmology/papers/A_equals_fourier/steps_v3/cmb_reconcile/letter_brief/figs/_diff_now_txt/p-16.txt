a full time-dependent light-cone calculation—evolving D(t), Rin (t), and Rout (t) from the present back through

ℓ ≥ 2 below ∼ 10−5 while the low-redshift A(ℓ) pattern remains Migkas-compatible. If a residual remains,

history, or a mild partner multipole) that removes it without reintroducing a fixed-curve conflict across redshift

5    Conclusions
Migkas et al. fix an off-centre CoE+shell—the donut-universe geometry with US at r = D—that fits directional
Hubble across redshift slices. Key Newtonian Ωs = 0.049 parameters are D ≈ 49.17 Gpc, Rin ≈ 34.48 Gpc,
Rout ≈ 70.27 Gpc, and ℓ0 = 13◦ ; the preferred relativistic headline is D ≈ 32.9 Gpc. Gravity on that geometry
predicts a last-scattering multipole whose kill function—an electromagnetic temperature multipole opposite in
sign and matching in angular shape—tracks the kinematic Doppler pattern from the same radii. Apparent
late-time acceleration is an illusion of off-centre sampling, requiring neither dark energy nor outward shell
acceleration; a contracting geometry would share the average appearance of acceleration but differ in angular
signature. The cosmological principle does not account for the Migkas anisotropy; the CMB isotropy remains an
independent constraint. The open problem is no longer whether the gravitational and electromagnetic shapes
talk to each other—they do—but whether a full time-dependent light-cone calculation can make them cancel
at the 10−5 level without spoiling the Migkas anisotropy. The goal is coexistence of both datasets under one
off-centre shell geometry.
