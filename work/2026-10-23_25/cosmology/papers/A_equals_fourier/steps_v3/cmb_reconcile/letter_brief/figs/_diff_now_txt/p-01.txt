                             from Migkas anisotropy to a flat CMB

                                                      Abstract
      Directional Hubble measurements from Migkas et al.[1] fix an off-centre Sedov–Taylor[2, 3] / Lemaı̂tre–
      Tolman–Bondi (LTB)[4, 5, 6] shell geometry about a Centre of Expansion (CoE), then checked against the
      CMB[7]. Both the Migkas sky-dependent Hubble rate and Planck isotropy of order 10−5 are taken as facts;
      the goal is coexistence, not replacement. The observer (US) sits off-centre inside a thick ordinary-matter
      shell—the donut-universe—and we ask whether the gravitational last-scattering multipole can be cancelled
      by a kill function: an electromagnetic ∆T /T multipole of kinematic–Doppler character, opposite in sign
      and matching in angular shape. Apparent late-time acceleration is an off-centre sampling illusion (no dark
      energy); a contracting geometry would share the average appearance but differ in angular signature.


1     Introduction
Migkas et al. report a directional cluster Hubble rate[1]; Planck finds CMB isotropy to ∼ 10−5 [7]. We treat both
as correct: Migkas anisotropy is free-fall about an off-centre CoE (Table 1; Figs. 2–4), and the CMB stress-tests
that geometry. Observer at r = D; anisotropy samples β(r), not a(t, θ). Open problem: keep Migkas A(ℓ)
while CMB multipoles stay ∼ 10−5 .
