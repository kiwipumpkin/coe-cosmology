Figure 8 shows the two-emitter baseline. If opposite
plates (or shells) share the observer’s bulk velocity, the
kinematic sky is flat before gravitational multipoles
are included.
In the infinite-plate idealisation the curvature radius
of each emitting surface is infinite: every line of sight
hits a flat wall, and solid angle is independent of
observer offset. Bringing that curvature radius down
from infinity toward the finite CoE–observer distance
D interpolates continuously from parallel plates to
concentric shells about the CoE. The shell (finite-R)
geometry is the physically relevant limit for last
scattering about a Centre of Expansion; the plate
limit is the pedagogical flat-sky baseline against which
Doppler residuals are compared.
The same off-centre geometry still produces a
gravitational multipole on the last-scattering sky. The
kill function is the electromagnetic temperature
multipole—∆T /T of kinematic–Doppler photon
character—required to cancel that gravitational
multipole: opposite in sign, same angular shape.
