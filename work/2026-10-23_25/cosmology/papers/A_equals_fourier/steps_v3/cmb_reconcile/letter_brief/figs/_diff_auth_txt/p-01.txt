                              Centre-of-Expansion shell cosmology:
                             from Migkas anisotropy to a flat CMB

                                                30 September 2026
Directional Hubble measurements from Migkas et al.[1] fix an off-centre Sedov–Taylor[2, 3] / Lemaı̂tre–Tolman–
Bondi (LTB)[4, 5, 6] shell geometry about a Centre of Expansion (CoE). The same geometry is then subjected
to an independent consistency check against the cosmic microwave background (CMB)[7]. This letter treats
both the Migkas sky-dependent cluster Hubble rate and the Planck-level CMB isotropy of order 10−5 as correct
empirical facts. The CoE is the geometric origin of the radial shell structure; the observer (US) sits off-
centre inside a thick ordinary-matter shell—the donut-universe configuration. We ask whether the gravitational
multipole implied by that placement can be cancelled by a kill function: an electromagnetic temperature
multipole (∆T /T of kinematic–Doppler character) opposite in sign to the gravitational last-scattering multipole
and sharing its angular shape. Apparent late-time accelerated expansion is, in this geometry, an illusion
requiring neither dark energy nor outward shell acceleration.


1     Problem and stance
Migkas et al. report a sky-dependent cluster Hubble rate. Standard cosmology assumes large-scale isotropy, and
the CMB is isotropic to ∼ 10−5 [7]. This letter takes both seriously: the cosmological principle alone does not
account for the Migkas anisotropy, and the goal is coexistence with a flat last-scattering sky, not replacement of
either dataset. The LTB origin is the CoE, not US; the observer sits at r = D, and anisotropy is an off-centre
sampling of β(r).
