logical agreement is unexpected if gravity and the photon bath are unrelated, and it suggests a geometric route
to CMB flatness that does not discard the Migkas fit. The open light-cone problem is whether the match can
be driven to the 10−5 amplitude required by Planck.
    Apparent late-time accelerated expansion is an illusion in this geometry: no dark energy and no actual out-
ward acceleration of the shell are required. Off-centre sampling of the expansion field produces the appearance.
A contracting universe under analogous sampling would also show, on average, the appearance of accelerated
expansion, but with a different angular signature—a natural observational distinguisher.


8     What we do next
    1. Evolve D(t), Rin (t), and Rout (t) from the present back through recombination, with the observer mid-
       shell.
    2. Integrate null geodesics on that history to obtain T (θ) on our sky, including gravitational and kinematic
       contributions together.
    3. Ask whether a shared bulk velocity of the emitting surfaces, plus the gravitational multipole, can keep
       ℓ ≥ 2 below ∼ 10−5 while the low-redshift A(ℓ) pattern remains Migkas-compatible.
    4. If a residual remains, identify the smallest additional degree of freedom (timing of recombination, shell
       thickness history, or a mild partner multipole) that removes it without reintroducing a fixed-curve conflict
       across redshift slices.


9     Bottom line
Migkas fixes an off-centre CoE+shell—the donut-universe geometry with US at r = D—that fits directional
Hubble across slices. Gravity on that geometry predicts a last-scattering multipole whose kill function—an
electromagnetic temperature multipole opposite in sign and matching in angular shape—tracks the kinematic
Doppler pattern from the same radii. Apparent late-time acceleration is an illusion of off-centre sampling; a
contracting geometry would share the average appearance but differ in angular signature. The open problem
is no longer whether the gravitational and electromagnetic shapes talk to each other—they do—but whether a
full time-dependent light-cone calculation can make them cancel at the 10−5 level without spoiling the Migkas
anisotropy. The goal is coexistence of both datasets under one off-centre shell geometry.
