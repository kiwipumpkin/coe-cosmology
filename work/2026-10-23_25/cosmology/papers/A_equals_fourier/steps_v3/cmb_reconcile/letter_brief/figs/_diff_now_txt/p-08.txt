2.5   Time evolution, donut universe, and CMB stress test
In the donut-universe picture the CoE is fixed while thick shells expand outward; the sphere of events visible
to us grows at c. Recombination is a spherical surface of fixed physical radius around the CoE. The observer
remains mid-shell between the inner and outer edges (S1 and S2). Critically, the Hubble-bubble volume about
US satisfies VHB = 0 until the observer passes Drecomb (the CoE distance at which vision turns on). After that
crossing, the visible sphere grows at c. Scale discussion may be framed in terms of the time-dependent volumes
VHB (t), VS1 (t), and VS2 (t) and the corresponding volume fractions of the Hubble bubble relative to the shell
annulus and the outer sphere. How long the post-recombination “oven” ran before an observer existed remains
an open timing question; the letter does not fix that interval, only the geometric sequence of shells crossing a
fixed recombination surface while Rvis grows at c.
