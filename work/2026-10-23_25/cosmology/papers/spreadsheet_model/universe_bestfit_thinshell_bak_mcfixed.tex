\begin{figure}[h]\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{universe_thin_drawing.png}
\caption{Three scales at the relativistic best fit; (b) and (c) are to scale in Gpc. (a) Hubble velocity vectors. In a Sedov--Taylor blast wave (Taylor 1950; Sedov 1959) $\vec V = k\,\times$(position from the CoE), so we move with $\vec V_O = k\vec D$ and a galaxy with $\vec V_P = k(\vec D+\vec r)$; then $\vec V_P-\vec V_O = k\vec r$ is Hubble's law, so $k = H$ and the CoE drops out. (b) Hubble bubble: ordinary-matter shell (grey, $\Omega_s=0.049$) from $R_{\rm in}=31.7$ to $R_{\rm out}=64.2$~Gpc around an empty interior and the CoE core; dashed circle $D=47.9$~Gpc through Earth; green vision sphere $R=14.3$~Gpc; Earth moves along $+x$ (anti-CoE, $\ell_0=13.5^\circ$) at $V_O=H_0D/c\approx11.2\,c$. (c) Cosmic shell: the core's Schwarzschild radius $r_s=538$~Gpc ($11.2\,D$); $r_s$ of all mass inside $D$ is 747~Gpc ($15.6\,D$), off scale. Blue arrows are velocities. The fit is exact relativistic (LTB), with ordinary matter only.}
\end{figure}
